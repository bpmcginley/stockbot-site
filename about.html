What this program is

Stockbot is a fully-automated systematic trading program written in C#. Once per trading day, just after the open (09:32 Eastern), it scores a universe of ~2,500 liquid US stocks and a basket of 31 multi-asset ETFs, decides a target portfolio, and rebalances a paper (simulated) brokerage account through the Alpaca API. No human touches the trades.

The book runs as independent sleeves that diversify each other:

sleevewhat it does
equity momentum corea genetic-algorithm-optimized ensemble of 3 momentum scorers picks ~10–15 liquid stocks with persistent, high-quality trends; rebalances monthly
ETF dual-momentumrotates a 31-ETF multi-asset basket (equities, bonds, gold, commodities, inverse indices) by relative + absolute momentum; parks in T-bills when nothing trends
BTC trenda small Bitcoin sleeve (via spot ETF) held only while BTC's own trend is positive, sized down when volatile
scraped overlaya bounded slice for fresh breakout candidates scraped daily from public screeners
defined-risk optionssells one small S&P index (XSP) put credit spread at a time, entered 75–90 days out, risk capped by the long leg; exits mechanically at a profit target, a stop, or 21 days to expiry

Capital is split between sleeves by inverse volatility (risk parity), and several independent risk layers — a market-regime filter, a crash-aware de-risker, a volatility target, a drawdown damper and a hard circuit breaker — can each pull the book toward cash. Un-deployed capital earns T-bill yield.

Every order also passes an automated compliance gate that classifies the instrument (equity, ETF, index option, …) and blocks anything outside the pre-approved set — unknown instruments fail closed.

Machine-learned self-improvement

A meta-labeling layer retrains itself monthly: a logistic model reads the market regime (recent returns, volatility ratios, trend, drawdown) and predicts whether tomorrow is a day the strategy tends to win. Its only power is to cut position size in half on the worst-looking days — it can never add exposure.

It is held to a pre-registered bar: purged, embargoed walk-forward cross-validation on the strategy's own out-of-sample record, requiring AUC ≥ 0.52. As of its latest monthly retrain the model failed that bar and is therefore inactive — it logs its opinion every day but touches nothing. If a future regime makes the features informative, it re-arms itself; until then, shipping the honest negative is the point. We do not tune features until a gate passes — that is how selection bias is born.

A second model learns an easier lesson from the first one's failure: instead of predicting direction (the hardest target in finance), a volatility forecaster predicts how rough the next month will be — volatility clusters, so this is genuinely learnable. It regresses forward one-month volatility on trailing volatility at three horizons plus cross-asset stress signals (credit spreads, treasuries, the dollar) that are not derived from the index itself. When it sees volatility rising faster than the trailing estimate has noticed, it can trim exposure early — never below half, never adding. It must pass two pre-registered gates before it touches anything: beat the naive forecast by 5%+ in purged walk-forward cross-validation, and beat it again across 60+ days of live shadow predictions logged before arming. It retrains monthly and disarms itself if its live accuracy degrades.

How it's validated

Every change to the strategy has to survive an honest gauntlet before it ships:

checkwhat it prevents
walk-forward out-of-sample testingeach period is traded by a model optimized ONLY on data before it — no peeking
multi-seed re-optimization A/Ba change must beat its control across many optimizer seeds, not in one lucky fit
deflated Sharpe + block-bootstrapcorrects for "we tried many things and kept the best" selection bias
deploy gates (WFE + alpha vs QQQ)a new champion is auto-rejected unless it generalizes AND adds skill over beta
parameter plateau checksa config only ships if its neighbors also work — knife-edge settings are luck

Ideas that failed these tests were dropped, and the failures are documented as carefully as the wins: leverage (all forms), tighter trailing stops, trend filters, residual momentum, a jump-risk "quality" filter, a mean-reversion sleeve, the ML sizing gate above, and a tail-put hedge all looked good somewhere and died out-of-sample.
